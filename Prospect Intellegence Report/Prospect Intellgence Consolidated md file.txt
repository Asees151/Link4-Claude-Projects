Consolidated Prospect Intelligence Report Skill
Overview

This skill generates a consolidated prospect intelligence report answering three questions:

"Who do I reach out to first, second, third?" (priority)
"Who can I actually get on the phone / get a reply from?" (reachability)
"What do I say, what do I avoid, and what's the goal?" (approach)
Outputs

3 deliverables:

Strategic Outreach Flowchart (DOCX) — Step 1→5 visual walkthrough. Step 1: The Company. Step 2: Which Link4 Product Fits. Step 3: Pain Points & Opportunity. Step 4: Who We Contact & How (with reachability + talking points). Step 5: Connection Path & Outreach Sequence. Contains critical questions per contact (3 per person max in this doc).
Detailed Intelligence Report (DOCX) — Full analysis per company with all sections including reachability, approach guidance, and connection path.
Conversation & Objection Guide (DOCX) — Separate document with ALL questions per contact ranked by channel (Cold Call / Walkthrough-Sales Meeting / Email), objections per contact with responses, and a channel priority ranking summary. Questions must be tied to each contact's specific position and responsibilities.

BOUNDARY: The Conversation Guide provides questions and objection handling. Full email scripts, LinkedIn message scripts, and cold call scripts belong in a separate outreach-playbook skill.

Workflow
STEP 0: COLLECT INPUTS

Confirm: companies, verticals (OEM/Modbus/Irrigation/Electrical/Greenhouse), End User vs Distributor, any known context. Do NOT assume.

STEP 1: RESEARCH EACH COMPANY

1a. Company Foundation — web_search + web_fetch: website, About, Products, LinkedIn, news 1b. Ownership & Financial — PE ownership, acquisitions, funding, revenue estimates 1c. Technology & Products — current systems, protocols, connected vs not 1d. Competitors — who they compete with, whether competitors offer what Link4 does 1e. Decision Makers — names, titles, tenure, LinkedIn, emails, phones (use Clay if connected) 1f. Timing Signals — hiring, expansion, leadership changes, trade shows 1g. Trade Shows & Associations (NEW) — check membership in industry groups (e.g., CANGC, AmericanHort, CSIA, AHR Expo). Association membership = trade show attendance = potential warm in-person meeting channel. Also check if they exhibit at relevant shows. 1h. Complete Contact Mapping (NEW) — beyond decision makers, identify: Purchasing/Finance contacts (PROCESS OWNERS who handle vendor onboarding), Gatekeepers (receptionists, assistants who answer phones), Operational staff who deal with the pain point daily (yard managers, field engineers, etc.)

STEP 2: ANALYZE & SCORE EACH COMPANY

2a. Company Profile Table — every field must cite its source 2b. Vertical Fit Assessment — why this vertical, distributor vs end user 2c. Buying Intent Score — using the scoring table (see below) 2d. Pain Points — 3-5 per company, each with: Pain Statement, Evidence, Evidence Type (✅/💡/❓), Link4 Solution, Impact Level, Discovery Question 2e. Competitor Analysis — products, IoT capability, gaps Link4 exploits 2f. Technology Stack — current systems, integration points 2g. Risk Assessment — deal breakers, headwinds, mitigations, overall level 2h. Timing Urgency — 1-5 scale with reasoning

2i. Reference Customer Positioning (NEW) For each prospect, determine how to position Link4's existing customer base:

If Link4 has customers in the SAME industry → lead with those references
If Link4 has customers in ADJACENT industries → position as "we work with commercial growers/manufacturers/integrators running similar operations" (this is honest and builds credibility)
If the prospect would be Link4's FIRST in their specific niche → position as "first partner" advantage: "preferential pricing, direct engineering attention, co-marketing opportunity"

For Irrigation (HydroRoot) specifically: Link4's greenhouse customers ARE commercial growers. When talking to nurseries, say "we work with commercial growers" — this is accurate. Greenhouses and nurseries overlap heavily on irrigation/water/zone management. Do NOT claim nursery-specific experience if none exists — say "growers running similar setups."

STEP 3: DECISION MAKER PROFILES

For each contact, build:

Contact Card: Name, Title, LinkedIn, Email (verified vs pattern — label which), Phone (direct vs main line), Location, Tenure

Strategic Profile:

Why This Person Matters (tied to verified facts)
What Motivates Them
Influence Map Position: DECISION MAKER / CHAMPION / TECHNICAL GATEKEEPER / PROCESS OWNER
Connection Points

Reachability Assessment (per contact, per channel):

Channel	Rating	What to consider
Cold Call	H/M/L	On-site vs traveling? Operational vs executive? Direct line vs switchboard? Gatekeeper? Industry norms?
Email	H/M/L	Verified vs pattern? C-suite gets flooded. Operational people may not check email.
LinkedIn	H/M/L	Active profile? Posts? Ag/trades/nursery people are usually NOT on LinkedIn.
In-Person	H/M/L	NEW. Is their site near Link4 (Anaheim)? Will-call or open facility? Can you show up with a physical unit? Ag/nursery people trust what they can see and touch.

Best Channel: Single most effective channel with reasoning.

LinkedIn ROI Check (NEW): If LinkedIn reachability is LOW or NONE for all contacts at a company, explicitly recommend reallocating LinkedIn effort to a higher-value activity (e.g., researching their plant availability lists, finding trade show attendance, or preparing a physical leave-behind for an in-person visit).

Gatekeeper Strategy (NEW): If the main phone line has a receptionist/gatekeeper:

Who is the gatekeeper? Name if known from staff page.
Suggested opener: Frame the call as a question, not a pitch. "Hi, I'm trying to reach [Name] — we work with nurseries on water management and I had a question about your irrigation setup. Is he/she available?"
If blocked: "No problem — could I leave a brief message? Or is there a better time to catch them?"
Do NOT say: "I'm calling about a product" or "Is the owner available?" — these trigger vendor-screening reflexes.

Talking Points (4 elements per contact):

Lead With: The one fact/angle most relevant to THIS person. Must reference a verified fact about their company. Frame as question or observation.
Do NOT Say: What would turn them off. Industry-specific mistakes to avoid.
If They Push Back: 2-3 likely objections with one-line responses (acknowledge → don't argue → redirect).
Question That Gets Them Talking: One open-ended question to get them sharing about current setup/pain.
STEP 3.5: CONVERSION GOALS & CONNECTION PATH (NEW)

Conversion Goals Per Channel:

Every channel has a specific, defined "win":

Channel	Conversion Goal	What Success Looks Like
Cold Call	Book a "walkthrough" (positioned as walkthrough, actually discovery) with the technical sales team (Drew/Dustin) on Zoom	Caller gets a confirmed date/time. Sends calendar invite before hanging up.
Email	Get a reply — any reply	Prospect responds. Then pivot to booking the walkthrough with sales team.
LinkedIn	Get engagement (accept + respond to message)	Then pivot to email or phone to book walkthrough.
In-Person	Leave a physical unit or one-pager, get a business card or verbal agreement to follow up	Opens the door for a scheduled walkthrough.

IMPORTANT — "Walkthrough" framing: The first meeting is positioned to the prospect as a "walkthrough" — casual, low-pressure, "let me show you how it works and ask about your setup." Internally, this IS the discovery call. The sales team (Drew/Dustin) runs it. Yen joins only if it's a CEO-level conversation or if it would add credibility. Do NOT call it a "discovery call" or "demo" to the prospect — "walkthrough" feels collaborative, not salesy.

Question rules for this document:

Every question MUST be tied to the contact's specific position and responsibilities
CEO gets capital/ROI/oversight questions
Operations/water person gets daily-reality/systems/monitoring questions
Technical person gets integration/compatibility questions
Sales person gets revenue/customer-demand questions
No generic questions that could apply to anyone
No nonsense questions — every question must reveal something that helps close the deal
Questions should make the contact feel like you understand their world

Objection rules:

Every objection must come from the contact's specific position (CEO worries about cost/risk, operations worries about disruption/complexity)
Response format: Acknowledge → Redirect → Never argue
Include the most likely pushback based on company research (e.g., family business = "we've been fine for X years")
