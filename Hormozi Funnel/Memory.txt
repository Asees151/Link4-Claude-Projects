Key learnings & principles

List quality precedes copy quality: Mismatched lead lists (mixing manufacturers, integrators, and service providers) undermine campaigns regardless of copy strength; segmentation must come before any rewrite
No customers = no proof → use substitutes: Without case studies, campaigns need credibility alternatives: pilot/risk-reversal offers, founder credentials, specific industry language, demo videos
Self-referential CTAs outperform broadcaster CTAs: Prompting prospects to calculate their own pain (vs. reading about someone else's results) drives stronger engagement
OEM service economics are real: Commercial refrigeration OEMs carry genuine warranty repair costs and aftermarket service revenue — Pearl's predictive alert value proposition is commercially grounded
Single vertical focus: Spreading messaging across pump, HVAC, and refrigeration simultaneously dilutes relevance; picking one vertical first is the right sequencing

Approach & patterns

Asees communicates frequently via voice-to-text, resulting in fragmented phrasing; prefers Claude to interpret intent and confirm before proceeding
Strong preference for extremely simple language: short sentences, no em dashes, third-grade reading level
Preferred format: one-liner description → bullet list of inputs/outputs → two-sentence outcome statement
Favors scannable bullets and outcome framing over dense paragraphs or tables
Iterates through multiple document versions with incremental refinement rather than wholesale rewrites
Asks clarifying definitional questions when terminology is unfamiliar (e.g., TCO, vendor comparison, truck rolls) — explanations should be brief and jargon-free
