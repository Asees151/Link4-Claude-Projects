1. THE CORE FOUR (ONLY WAYS TO GET LEADS)

There are exactly four ways to get leads. Everything else is a variation of one of these four. The 2×2 matrix:

	1-to-1	1-to-many
Warm audience	Warm Outreach	Free Content
Cold audience	Cold Outreach	Paid Ads

Definitions:

Lead: A person you can contact
Engaged lead: A person who shows interest in what you sell
Warm audience: People who already know you exist
Cold audience: Strangers

The one reason you don't have enough leads: "You're not advertising enough. Period."

Not wrong strategy. Not wrong market. Not wrong product. You're simply not getting your offer in front of enough people.

Start with the one that matches your current assets:

Have an audience? → Content first
Have contacts? → Warm outreach first
Have cash? → Paid ads
Have neither? → Cold outreach (hardest, but always available)
2. WARM OUTREACH PROTOCOL

The easiest and cheapest lead source. "Everybody knows somebody."

The 10-Step Warm Outreach System:

Scrape ALL contacts across platforms (phone, email, LinkedIn, IG, FB, etc.) — aim for 1,000+
Pick the platform with the most contacts
Send personalized messages — find what's happening in their life first
100 reach-outs per day, first 4 hours of the workday, attempt each contact up to 3 times
Use the ACA Framework in messaging:
Acknowledge: Restate what's happening in their life
Compliment: Praise something specific and genuine
Ask: Lead toward your offer with a question, not a pitch
Don't ask them to buy first — ask if they know anyone: "By the way, do you know anyone who [struggles with X] looking to [dream outcome] in [timeframe]? I'm taking on 5 case studies and working with them for free in exchange for a testimonial."
Offer free work for testimonials when starting out
Once you exhaust one platform, move to the next
Gradually raise prices every 5 new customers
Keep the list warm with regular value (not just pitches)

Why this works: People who know you have a baseline of trust. You're not starting from zero. The referral ask ("do you know anyone?") is less threatening than a direct pitch.

3. CONTENT / FREE VALUE STRATEGY

"Give away the secrets. Sell the implementation."

Only ~1% of people who consume free content will take action and need your help. The other 99% need encouragement, accountability, and hand-holding — that's the paid offer.

The foundational belief: "When your free stuff is better than their paid stuff, what can they do?" "Look at what everyone else does. Do it better. Make it free. Monetize another way."

The Accordion Framework (volume to quality):

Start with HIGH volume (you're bad at first — make up for quality with quantity)
Look at your top 10% performing content
Do more of what works (expand the accordion)
Double down on winners (contract — do fewer, better)
Look at the new top 10% of that better content
Expand again Quantity teaches you what quality looks like. Quality is learned, not innate.

Every content piece follows Hook-Retain-Reward:

Hook: Grab attention. Shock, provocative question, bold promise, or pattern interrupt. "My advertising became 20x more effective when I focused the majority of effort on the first 5 seconds."
Retain: Keep engagement. Lists, steps, stories with stakes, open loops.
Reward: Deliver fully on the hook's promise. Actionable, specific, memorable.

"How I" beats "How To": "How I went from $0 to $1M in 18 months" > "How to make your first million" Personal experience > generic advice. Always.

Platform strategy:

Test ideas on Twitter/X first (fast feedback, low cost)
Collect best-performing tweets
Turn winners into short-form video scripts
Best short-form becomes long-form YouTube
Long-form sliced into 30+ pieces across platforms
"Working once, winning forever."

Volume numbers from Hormozi's team:

35,000+ pieces of content in 40 months
~80 pieces per week published across platforms
$4M invested in content creation
Result: 7.8M+ followers across platforms, 1.2M added in 6 months at peak

The quality metric shift: Don't optimize for views. Optimize for RPM (revenue per thousand impressions). An audience of 10,000 ideal buyers > an audience of 1,000,000 casual scrollers.

4. COLD OUTREACH PROTOCOL

The hardest of the Core Four but always available. Zero cost to start. Just time and volume.

The fundamental challenge: Strangers don't trust you. You must compensate with:

Personalization — act like you know them. Research before reaching out.
Big Fast Value — give something genuinely valuable in the first message. "Blow their minds in under 30 seconds. Give away something free people would normally pay for."

Volume reality:

1-5% of people contacted become engaged leads (industry standard)
Contact minimum 100 people per day for 100 days
"The biggest mistake is dramatically underestimating the volume required."

The readability hack: "I rewrote our cold outreach template to below a third-grade reading level. Results: 50% more leads responded." Short sentences. Simple words. No jargon.

Cold outreach sequence:

Research: find one specific, genuine thing about them to reference
Open: acknowledge something personal (not generic flattery)
Bridge: connect their situation to the problem you solve
Offer: a free resource, insight, or assessment — not a pitch
CTA: one clear next step. Never more than one.

When cold outreach doesn't work: It's almost always a volume problem, not a message problem. "Did it not work, or did you just stop too early?"

5. PAID ADS PHILOSOPHY

"The reach is guaranteed. Getting your money back isn't. It's a game of efficiency."

The fundamental ad formula: [Call Out] + [Value / Hook] + [Call to Action]

"My advertising became 20x more effective when I focused the majority of effort on the first 5 seconds." The hook is everything. Test hooks relentlessly.

Testing philosophy: Spend $100 on 10 ad variants. Nine lose all $100. One makes $500. You're down $500 net. Most people stop here. Wrong. "You found a winner. Now 100x that winner."

Small losses on tests are the price of finding winners that pay for everything.

The economics of paid ads: The cost to acquire customers is much closer between competitors than people think. The difference between winners and losers is how much they make per customer (LTGP). "They think they have bad ads (high CAC) when they actually have a bad business model (low LTGP)."

Fix the economics first, then scale ads: If LTV:CAC is below 3:1, no amount of ad optimization will save you. Fix the offer, the upsells, the continuity, the retention — then pour gas on it.

Platform agnosticism: Don't optimize for algorithms — optimize for the platform's fundamental goal. YouTube wants clicks AND watch time → make people click and stay. If you satisfy the platform's deepest goal, distribution follows. Algorithm tricks are noise.

When to scale:

LTV:CAC ratio above 3:1 (target 6-9:1 for service businesses)
Clear winner ad identified and stable
Can handle the lead volume operationally
Payback period within 30 days
6. THE LEAD MAGNET FRAMEWORK

Definition: A complete solution to a narrow problem that, once solved, reveals another problem your core offer solves.

The bar analogy: Salty pretzels solve hunger AND create thirst — solved by a drink (for money). A great lead magnet solves one problem AND reveals the next.

The golden rule: "Provide more value than the cost of your core offer before they've bought it." And: "Grand Slam Offers work for free stuff as much or better than paid stuff."

Three Types of Lead Magnets

1. Reveal the Problem (Diagnosis) Website speed tests. Posture assessments. Free audits. Termite inspections. Works best when: the problem gets worse over time without intervention.

2. Samples and Trials Full but limited access — by uses or time. Free first session, free first week, free first module. Works best when: your offer solves a recurring problem and usage builds habit.

3. One Step of a Multi-Step Process Give one valuable step free, charge for the rest. The book IS the lead magnet: it teaches up to $1M/year, then reveals that Acquisition.com solves the next set of problems.

Four Lead Magnet Delivery Methods
Software/tools: calculators, dashboards, assessments
Information: mini-courses, checklists, frameworks, tips
Services: free audits, consultations, assessments
Physical products: books, samples, starter kits

Three types × four delivery methods = up to 12 possible lead magnets per narrow problem.

Lead magnet economics: Without lead magnet: $1,000 per call, close 1/3 = $3,000 CAC With lead magnet: $100 per call, 1/10 buy = $1,000 CAC 3x cheaper customer acquisition.

7. LEAD GETTERS (SCALING BEYOND PERSONAL EFFORT)

The Core Four yields linear growth. Lead Getters yield exponential growth. You're getting OTHER people to advertise for you.

Type 1: Customer Referrals

When Hormozi's ad account shut down, they didn't notice for two weeks — making $500K/week from referrals alone. Word-of-mouth at scale is the ultimate business.

Two reasons you don't get referrals:

Product isn't good enough
You never ask

"Treat the referral request like an offer — show customers the value they get from referring."

How to ask: "I only take new clients through referrals. Who's the one person in your network who needs what you just got? I'll give them [X] and I'll give you [Y] for the introduction."

Type 2: Employees / Team

Trade 40 hours of doing for 4 hours of managing. Net 36 hours. But only works if you document the process so anyone can follow it. "You can't delegate a process you haven't documented."

Type 3: Agencies

Hormozi's unconventional approach: "I hire agencies not to outsource permanently, but to learn. I work with them for 6 months, learn how they do it, then train my internal team. Once we can do it ourselves, I switch to a consulting arrangement." Use agencies as paid education, not permanent dependencies.

Type 4: Affiliates & Partners

The most powerful multiplier. An affiliate is an independent business that tells their audience to buy your stuff.

The math: Selling 10 customers/month yourself = $100K/month = $1.2M/year Getting 10 affiliates who each bring 1 customer/month:

Month 1: 10 customers
Each affiliate's audience keeps growing
You keep acquiring more affiliates
Year 1 total: ~$7.8M (and accelerating)

Finding affiliates: Think of the Dream 100 — 100 businesses whose audiences perfectly overlap with your ideal customer. Build relationships with them. Give them an irresistible structure (leads, revenue share, cross-promotion).

The Whisper-Tease-Shout Launch:

Whisper: Post content, reach out to friends, create curiosity without revealing
Tease: Reveal the product, show value elements, build anticipation
Shout: Pound the audience with bonuses, scarcity, urgency, and direct CTAs
8. THE RULE OF 100

"Commit to 100 primary actions every day for 100 days."

"I don't make many promises. But this is one: commit to the Rule of 100 and you will never go hungry again."

Applied:

100 warm reach-outs per day
100 minutes of content creation per day
100 cold reach-outs per day
$100/day minimum in ad spend

"Volume negates luck."

The Rule of 100 eliminates the question of whether your approach "works." If you've done 10,000 primary actions and still have nothing, THEN you have a messaging or offer problem. Until then, you just haven't done enough.

9. THE MORE / BETTER / NEW FRAMEWORK

When you want more leads, go in this exact order:

1. MORE (before anything else) Crank up volume to maximum capacity. Doubling inputs often doubles outputs. Most businesses that think they have a lead gen problem just need more volume of what's working. "Did you try it enough?" The answer is almost always no.

2. BETTER (once More is maxed) Test one thing per week per channel. Identify where leads drop off in the funnel — that's the bottleneck. Optimize that one thing relentlessly before touching anything else.

3. NEW (only after exhausting More and Better) New placements, new platforms, new Core Four activities. Starting with "New" is almost always wrong — you're adding complexity before understanding the basics.

The common mistake: Jumping to "New" when the problem is just "More." Entrepreneurs love new. They try TikTok before they've maxed out Instagram. They launch a podcast before they've maxed out their email list. Novelty is the enemy of compounding.

10. BUSINESS MATH FOR LEAD GEN

The key question: Is this getting me more customers cheaper, or making those customers worth more? If not, stop doing it.

LTGP:CAC targets:

3:1 minimum (automated businesses)
6:1 for service businesses with some human touch
9:1 for high-touch service businesses
Below 3:1 = broken economics. No amount of volume fixes it.

Payback period: Recoup CAC within 30 days. This is a cash flow imperative. Structure offers to collect cash upfront: annual plans, deposits, pay-in-full incentives.

The lead gen math check: "If I spend $X on [channel] today, what am I getting back, and when?"

Do you know your cost per lead?
Do you know your lead-to-sale conversion rate?
Do you know your average transaction value and LTV? If the answer to any of these is "no" → build the measurement first.
11. CONTENT STRATEGY DEEP DIVE

The content hierarchy (where to invest time):

Short-form video (TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts) — highest reach per unit
Long-form video (YouTube) — highest trust and conversion per viewer
Written (Twitter/X, LinkedIn) — fastest to test, best for idea validation
Email — highest ROI for engaged audiences (owned channel, not rented)
Podcast — lowest production-to-impact ratio for most businesses starting out

Content types that convert (in order):

Proof content (client results, before/after, testimonials)
Personal story content (failure, struggle, specific lesson)
Framework content (named system, step-by-step, checklist)
Contrarian content (disagrees with conventional wisdom with evidence)
Inspirational/motivational content (lowest conversion — use sparingly)

The 80/20 of content: 80% give away tactics and frameworks. 20% demonstrate results and proof. The tactics build trust. The proof converts.

Measuring content that matters: Not views. Not followers. Not likes. → Opt-ins per week (direct lead gen) → Revenue per thousand impressions (monetization efficiency) → Sales mentions ("I found you through your content on...")

The long game: "Building an audience is the most valuable thing I've ever done." In any given month, Hormozi's organic content generates more qualified leads than his paid ads. But it took years to build. You can't shortcut compounding.

The content-to-capital flywheel (Acquisition.com model): Free content → builds authority → attracts business owners → workshops ($5K) → masterminds ($35K) → equity investments → portfolio grows → more proof → better content → more authority → repeat
